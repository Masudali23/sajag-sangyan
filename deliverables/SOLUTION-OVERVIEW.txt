SAJAG: UNDERSTAND BEFORE YOU ACT
Solution overview, SANGYAN Investor Resilience Hackathon

Team FirstBest: Anshuman Biswal · Masud Ali
Tracks: Track C (Investor Education for Bharat) and Track E (Misinformation & Financial Content Literacy)
Version 2.0 · Website: https://sajag-ashen.vercel.app/ · Android: https://sajag-ashen.vercel.app/downloads/Sajag-Android-Debug.apk


1. PROBLEM AND USER

Asha, a first-time investor in a Tier-2 town, receives a confident financial forward in her family group: "Har mahine 8% pakka return. Join our VIP group today; only 5 places left." A simple "true/false" badge would not tell her why the message is risky, and it would not help her judge the next one. Financial terms such as NAV, risk, volatility and fees are usually explained in English, for people who already understand them.

We built Sajag for first-time investors in Tier-2 and Tier-3 cities, families who receive forwarded offers, people more at home in Hindi or Bengali, and anyone who prefers listening to reading.


2. OUR SOLUTION

Sajag connects checking with learning in one journey:

Message → purpose and quoted warning signs → evidence and gaps → official guidance → everyday analogy → short question → fictional consequence.

- Check a message: paste, type, share or speak a message. The first check runs on the device and needs no internet.
- Understand the warning: Sajag quotes the exact words that raise concern, says whether the wording reads as promotion or education, explains what evidence is missing, and links the relevant SEBI, RBI or government guidance.
- Learn the idea behind it: every warning leads to a short lesson, a fictional scam story or a money simulator.

Sajag never gives a "safe" verdict. Results are "Potential", "Strong" or "Not enough evidence", with the uncertainty explained.


3. HOW SAJAG FITS TRACK C AND TRACK E

Track E: Misinformation & Financial Content Literacy
- Claim evidence-checker: quotes the exact words behind each warning, shows the evidence and gaps (who guarantees a return, under what conditions, at what cost), and links official guidance. It is never a binary true/false verdict.
- Promotion vs education: each result carries a purpose label ("Promotional cues", "Explanatory wording", "Education + promotion" or "Purpose unclear") with the words that led to it.

Track C: Investor Education for Bharat
- Voice-first explainer: 15 lessons with everyday analogies, takeaways and a quiz. Seven cover the terms named in the track brief (risk, diversification, volatility, compounding, fees, NAV and nomination), and eight explain how common scams work. All are in English, Hindi and Bengali, and "Listen" reads them aloud.
- Consequence simulator: four fictional chat stories ("digital arrest" call, part-time "like" job, VIP stock-tips group, cashback QR code) show each pressure tactic step by step. The money simulator uses fictional tokens: a 20% fall turns 10,000 into 8,000, and getting back to 10,000 needs a 25% rise. With 10,000 borrowed, the same fall leaves 6,000 after the debt is repaid. Zero real money is involved, and these are arithmetic examples, not forecasts.


4. KEY FEATURES

- Three interface languages: English, Hindi and Bengali (Bengali in beta, pending native-speaker review). Messages can also be written in Hinglish and Banglish, five tested ways of writing in all.
- Voice: "Speak it" turns speech into editable text before checking. "Listen" reads results, lessons and stories aloud for people who prefer listening. Both use the phone's or browser's speech services, so available voices vary by device. Voice notes forwarded from chat apps are not imported.
- Offline first: after the first sign-in and a complete download, the on-device check, lessons, stories and simulators work without internet.
- Optional AI review: only when the user allows it for that message.
- Accounts: sign up with email and a password, verify the email once with a code, then sign in with the password. Each account has its own notebook, and saving or cloud backup happens only when the user chooses.
- Android app 2.0 (Android 7.0 or later) and a responsive website. On Android, Back closes any open menu first, then returns to Home.


5. TECHNOLOGY AND ARCHITECTURE

Two layers, one purpose.

On the device, always on:
- Context-aware rules (more than 800 reviewed examples in development) plus a small statistical pattern model, written in TypeScript and shared by the website and Android app. Together they detect pressure tactics, payment and credential requests, guaranteed-return claims and selling incentives, and quote the exact words.

Online, only with the user's permission for that message:
1. Personal details are masked (best effort).
2. Retrieval (RAG): a BM25 index selects passages from 22 curated SEBI, RBI and government guidance cards.
3. Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite reads the message with that guidance in context.
4. Every AI warning must quote the message exactly and match compatible guidance.
5. A second pass confirms or withdraws each proposed warning.

The AI can add warnings and can clear eligible on-device false alarms. A cleared warning stays visible as context. Strong results cannot be withdrawn, and if the AI review cannot finish, the on-device result stands.

Stack: React + Vite web app, Capacitor Android app, Express API on Vercel, Supabase Auth and opt-in notebook backup, Google Gemini API. Components and licences are listed in THIRD-PARTY.txt.


6. RESULTS

Held-out test: 150 synthetic messages (75 risky and 75 harmless) across English, Hindi, Bengali, Hinglish and Banglish, scored once per mode, separate from the messages used in development.

                                Offline (on device)    With AI review
Correct                         124 / 150 (82.7%)      149 / 150 (99.3%)
Risky messages flagged          57 / 75                74 / 75
Harmless messages flagged       8 / 75                 0 / 75

By writing form (correct, offline → with AI): English 83.3% → 100%, Hindi 83.3% → 96.7%, Bengali 83.3% → 100%, Hinglish 80.0% → 100%, Banglish 83.3% → 100%.

The AI review completed for 145 of 150 messages. The other five used the on-device result, and they are included in the AI figures. No harmless message received a Strong result in either mode.

The test messages were synthetic, written for testing before tuning; the full set is in datasets/evaluation/holdout-150.json. The figures measure whether a message received a warning, not the scam category or real-world losses prevented, and they are not a real-world guarantee.


7. PRIVACY AND GUARDRAILS

- No stock tips, buy/sell/hold advice, price predictions, broker or product promotion, commissions or upsells.
- No SMS reading and no OTP or bank details collected. The email code is only for verifying a Sajag account; Sajag never asks for a bank, card or UPI code.
- Permission before every AI check: the user sees what will be shared. Only the masked message is sent, and masking is best effort.
- Trade-offs we state openly: AI submissions follow Google's data terms (the free tier may use them to improve its products), and the notebook saved on the device is not encrypted.
- Guidance explains warning patterns. It does not verify a sender, a registration or a link.


8. HOW SAJAG MEETS THE JUDGING CRITERIA

- Investor resilience and safety (30%): flags risky messages before money moves and explains why. With AI, 74 of 75 risky and 0 of 75 harmless test messages were flagged. Lessons and stories give practice for safer responses.
- Bharat-first usability and accessibility (25%): Hindi and Bengali screens, Hinglish and Banglish input, Listen and Speak it, offline core features and a 5.2 MB Android app.
- Trust, privacy and guardrail compliance (15%): uncertainty shown instead of "safe", sources linked, permission per AI check, no tips, promotion or monetisation.
- Technical execution and feasibility (15%): a working website and Android app, on-device rules and pattern model, RAG over curated official guidance with quote checks and a second review pass.
- Impact and scalability (15%): the on-device layer costs nothing per check, the optional AI layer uses a low-cost model, and new languages and guidance cards plug into the same pipeline.


9. IMPACT AND NEXT STEPS

Sajag turns a moment of risk into a moment of learning: warning, explanation and practice together. Because the core runs on the device, it can serve users with slow or patchy internet at almost no cost per check.

Next:
- Test with consenting regional-language users, including older and first-time investors, and measure comprehension, accessibility and safer choices.
- Evaluate on a wider range of real scam messages.
- Add more Indian languages and guidance cards.
- Measure where Sajag helps, where it fails, and whether it reduces losses. We do not yet claim a reduction in financial loss.

Sajag gives education and warning signs, not investment advice.
